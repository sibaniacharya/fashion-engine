GRADUATION PROJECT — AI-POWERED WISHLIST DISCOVERY ENGINE

PRODUCT

Chosen product: Myntra

Role:
Product Manager on the Growth Team at Myntra.

BUSINESS CONTEXT

Millions of users browse fashion products, save products they like, and add products to their wishlists.

A wishlist represents an important user signal because the user has explicitly expressed interest in a product but has not yet purchased it.

Over time, users can accumulate dozens or hundreds of wishlisted products, while only a small proportion may eventually result in purchases.

The company's strategic goal is:

Increase the percentage of users who purchase at least one item from their wishlist within 30 days of adding it.

Improving wishlist-to-purchase conversion could:
- increase purchase frequency
- improve monetization from existing users
- increase the value generated from existing high-intent demand

IMPORTANT CONSTRAINT

The final product solution cannot offer monetary incentives to users.

The underlying user problem is NOT provided.

The objective is to discover the underlying problem through research and evidence.


==================================================
PART 1 — AI-POWERED DISCOVERY ENGINE
==================================================

Before proposing any product solution, build an AI-powered Discovery Engine that analyzes user feedback at scale.

The Discovery Engine should analyze publicly available conversations and feedback related to online fashion shopping.

Potential sources include:

- App Store reviews
- Google Play reviews
- Reddit discussions
- Fashion and shopping communities
- Social media conversations
- YouTube comments
- Product reviews
- Product Q&A
- Other publicly available conversations about online fashion shopping

The system does NOT need to use every possible source.

The architecture should support multiple sources, but the initial implementation should prioritize sources that are legally and technically accessible and provide useful evidence.

Do not fabricate data.

Do not scrape behind authentication.

Do not bypass CAPTCHA, WAF, login restrictions, or Terms of Service.

Use legitimate public APIs, exports, datasets, or permitted public access methods.


==================================================
DISCOVERY QUESTIONS
==================================================

The Discovery Engine should help answer:

1. Why do users add fashion products to their wishlist?

2. What prevents wishlisted products from eventually being purchased?

3. What uncertainties remain after users have identified a product they like?

4. What causes users to postpone a purchase?

5. How do users compare multiple shortlisted products?

6. What information do users seek outside Myntra before purchasing?

7. What role do the following factors play?

- fit
- size
- styling
- price
- reviews
- quality
- occasion
- social validation

8. When is the wishlist used as:

- genuine purchase intent
- active evaluation
- future purchase intent
- bookmarking
- inspiration

9. How do these behaviors differ across user segments?

10. What unmet needs appear consistently across user conversations?

11. What purchase barriers appear repeatedly?

12. What information is missing at the point where users decide whether to purchase?

13. What workarounds do users currently use?

14. What happens outside the Myntra app before users make their final purchase decision?

DISCOVERY ENGINE OBJECTIVE

The system must go beyond:

- simple review summarization
- sentiment analysis
- star-rating analysis
- keyword frequency

The system should identify, quantify where possible, compare, and prioritize potential opportunity areas that could influence wishlist-to-purchase conversion.

==================================================
DATA INGESTION
==================================================

Create a modular ingestion architecture.

Initial sources may include:

1. Google Play reviews
2. Reddit
3. YouTube comments
4. Public fashion/product reviews and Q&A

The architecture should allow additional sources to be added later, including Apple App Store reviews where permitted.

Each source should have an independent adapter.

Normalize records into a common schema containing, where available:

- internal_id
- source
- source_id
- date
- title
- text
- rating
- URL
- category
- metadata


==================================================
DATA CLEANING
==================================================

The cleaning pipeline should:

- remove exact duplicates
- detect near duplicates where practical
- remove empty records
- identify spam
- identify irrelevant content
- detect language
- normalize whitespace
- normalize metadata
- remove or mask PII
- preserve original text
- preserve source provenance

IMPORTANT:

Do NOT automatically remove all reviews with fewer than 8 words.

Short reviews can contain valuable purchase signals.

Examples:

"Should I size up?"

"Is this transparent?"

"Anyone tried this?"

"Does it fit well?"

Meaningful short feedback must be retained.

Do NOT remove meaningful text merely because it contains emojis.

Emoji-only or meaningless content may be removed.

Do not silently discard non-English feedback.

Language should be detected and recorded.

The initial AI analysis may focus on English if necessary, but excluded languages and counts must be documented.


==================================================
AI SIGNAL EXTRACTION
==================================================

For each normalized feedback item, identify signals where supported by the actual text.

Potential fields include:

- user_segment
- shopping_intent
- wishlist_intent
- purchase_stage
- pain_point
- uncertainty
- purchase_barrier
- information_sought
- comparison_behavior
- fit_size_signal
- styling_signal
- price_signal
- quality_signal
- review_social_validation_signal
- occasion_signal
- external_research_behavior
- theme_candidate
- evidence_strength

The AI must not invent unsupported information.

If a signal cannot be determined from the text, return null or unknown.

The system must preserve evidence traceability so that important findings can be linked back to real feedback.


==================================================
THEME DISCOVERY
==================================================

Themes should be discovered from the data rather than being completely hard-coded.

The system should:

- identify recurring patterns
- cluster semantically similar feedback
- generate candidate themes
- merge highly similar themes
- remove meaningless clusters
- calculate frequency
- calculate percentage
- compare themes across sources
- compare themes across user segments

The final system should identify a manageable number of meaningful themes.

Approximately 8–12 themes may be generated before prioritization.

The system should retain evidence supporting each theme.


==================================================
WISHLIST BEHAVIOR ANALYSIS
==================================================

Analyze the shopping journey:

Discovery
→ Product Interest
→ Wishlist
→ Evaluation
→ Comparison
→ Purchase / Postponement / Abandonment

Distinguish between:

1. Wishlist as bookmarking

2. Wishlist as inspiration

3. Wishlist as future purchase intent

4. Wishlist as active evaluation

5. Wishlist as genuine purchase intent

6. Wishlist followed by comparison

7. Wishlist followed by postponement

8. Wishlist followed by abandonment

Do not assume that every wishlist mention represents strong purchase intent.


==================================================
PURCHASE BARRIER ANALYSIS
==================================================

Identify barriers that may prevent a user from moving from wishlist to purchase.

Potential barriers may include:

- fit uncertainty
- size uncertainty
- quality uncertainty
- styling uncertainty
- price uncertainty
- lack of social validation
- lack of reviews
- occasion uncertainty
- product comparison
- uncertainty about whether the product will look as expected
- uncertainty about material
- uncertainty about return/replacement
- waiting for more information
- seeking external validation

These are examples, NOT predetermined answers.

The actual data should determine which barriers matter.


==================================================
EXTERNAL INFORMATION SEEKING
==================================================

Identify what users do outside the shopping platform before purchasing.

Examples may include:

- searching Google
- checking Instagram
- watching YouTube
- reading Reddit
- checking other marketplaces
- asking friends
- checking influencer content
- looking for size/fit reviews
- comparing products elsewhere

Only classify behaviors that are supported by evidence.

Do not infer external research without supporting evidence.


==================================================
USER SEGMENTATION
==================================================

Identify meaningful user segments where possible.

Potential segments may include:

- students
- young professionals
- frequent fashion shoppers
- occasional shoppers
- budget-conscious shoppers
- trend-driven shoppers
- users with high fit/size concerns

These are hypotheses only.

The data should determine whether meaningful segments can actually be identified.

Compare:

- wishlist behavior
- purchase barriers
- themes
- information-seeking behavior

across segments where sufficient evidence exists.


==================================================
OPPORTUNITY IDENTIFICATION
==================================================

The system should identify potential opportunity areas related to:

Wishlist → Purchase within 30 days.

An opportunity should represent:

USER PROBLEM
+
BEHAVIORAL BARRIER
+
POTENTIAL PRODUCT OUTCOME

Do NOT turn an opportunity directly into a product solution.

For each opportunity evaluate:

- frequency
- wishlist relevance
- potential purchase impact
- user pain
- cross-source consistency
- evidence confidence

Use transparent scoring.

Opportunity scoring should be calculated programmatically rather than relying entirely on an LLM-generated score.

Each opportunity should include:

- opportunity_name
- problem_statement
- affected_segments
- journey_stage
- frequency
- wishlist_relevance
- purchase_impact
- user_pain
- cross_source_consistency
- evidence_confidence
- opportunity_score
- supporting_evidence
- key_uncertainty


==================================================
EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS
==================================================

The Discovery Engine must be evidence-based.

Do not fabricate:

- reviews
- quotes
- statistics
- themes
- user behaviors
- user segments
- opportunity evidence

Every important finding should be traceable to actual collected feedback.

Representative quotes must be actual user-generated text.

Remove or mask PII before displaying quotes.
